World Map

A world map is a map of most or all of the surface of Earth. World maps, because of their scale, must deal with the problem of projection. Maps rendered in two dimensions by necessity distort the display of the three-dimensional surface of the earth. While this is true of any map, these distortions reach extremes in a world map. Many techniques have been developed to present world maps that address diverse technical and aesthetic goals

A continent is any of several large landmasses. Generally identified by convention rather than any strict criteria, up to seven geographical regions are commonly regarded as continents. Ordered from largest in area to smallest,these seven regions are: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, and Australia. Variations with fewer continents may merge some of these, for example America, Eurasia, or Afro-Eurasia are sometimes treated as single continents, which can bring the total number as low as four. Zealandia, a largely submerged mass of continental crust, has also been described as a continent.

Equirectangular Projection Equirectangular map projection is an equidistant cylindrical projection with the standard parallel located at the equator. It is one of the simplest and oldest map projections found by Marinus of Tyre around A.D. 100. It's usage was more common in the past. The projection is neither equal-area nor conformal. Because of the distortions introduced by this projection, it has little use in navigation or cadastral mapping and finds its primary use in thematic mapping. Cartographers can use this projection for simple portrayals of the world or regions with minimal geographic data and those not quiring accurate areas. Simple Portrayal makes the projection useful for index maps and to map phenomena that change with longitude, for example,time zones. The forward projection transforms spherical coordinates into planar coordinates. The reverse projection transforms from the plane back onto the sphere.
